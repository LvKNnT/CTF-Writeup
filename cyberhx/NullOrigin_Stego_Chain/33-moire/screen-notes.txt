PROCESS ROOM - SCREEN NOTES - JOB 4471
======================================

  Ruling as ordered. The screen is set FOUR TO THE CELL, and the
  cell is square. Every cell on the plate is laid to a weight and
  the weight is the number of dots that fall black in it.

  We do not alter a weight. Alter a weight and the picture goes
  soft in that spot, and the overseer has an eye for it, and then
  there are questions in the yard. The weight is sacred.

  But a cell of a given weight can be SET more than one way, and
  a cell set one way and a cell set the other way weigh exactly
  the same, and print exactly the same to any man looking at the
  sheet from a foot away.

  Only the middle weights have room to move. A cell that is very
  light or very black can only be set the one way and we leave
  those alone. Between those two there is a choice, and where
  there is a choice there is a hand.

  The machine lays the cells as the ox ploughs. Down one furrow,
  and back along the next, and down again.

  Which of the two settings means which was not the compositor's
  to decide. The card decides that, as the card decides
  everything on this line.

  Do not look for it in the sheet. It is not in the sheet. It is
  in what the sheet had to be broken into before a press could
  take it.

  And do not have the sheet re-screened. A sheet that has been
  through the process room twice has been set by whoever ran it
  the second time, and his hand is not the hand you are reading.
